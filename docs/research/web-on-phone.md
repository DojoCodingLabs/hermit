# Earn the phone one web family at a time

Hermit cannot match Atomic, Claude Duet, ChatGPT Dots, Grok Bot, Instinct and Meta Muse on open-ended web tasks with a sub-1B model today. It can, however, cover most of what users buy and look up through a phone-first stack that keeps the model's job small. The stack has five parts: search and lookups where code does the crawling, structured commerce paths (Shopify Shop MCP, Instacart and Uber MCP, x402 with Tether WDK), WebMCP capture in the WebView, recipes replayed without the model, and a dedicated small web-step model that only picks among a few candidates inside a skill. The evidence sets the limits plainly. **The smallest published live-web agent is 4B** (Fara1.5-4B at 57.3% on Online-Mind2Web, a vendor claim). The smallest credible multi-step results are 2B on Android and 3B on WebArena-Lite. **No web navigation result below 2B has been published**, and the 2025 to 2026 slope (2B Android agents went from about 8% to about 56% success) says the gap is closing faster than the repo's earlier "not viable" verdict assumed. The competitors themselves are frontier models on cloud VMs that pay through scoped tokens and ask before every purchase, and none publishes a task-success number. Parity therefore has to be defined per task family and measured on Hermit's own cases. Each family should move from Boost to the phone only when the phone passes the same frozen eval Boost passes, at pass^4, with zero spend or credential violations under adaptive injection and a P90 latency bar. That is the roadmap PDR-026 asks for, and its central experiment, a published sub-1B web navigation result, would be a first.

## Parity today is a frontier model on someone else's computer

Every leading consumer agent runs a frontier cloud model that drives a Chromium browser on a cloud machine. **Meta Muse** browses and fills forms in a dedicated cloud Chromium and pays through Link by Stripe. Meta calls it the first AI agent covered by Link's purchase protections, and it confirms before purchases ([TechRepublic](https://www.techrepublic.com/article/news-meta-muse-ai-agent-us-launch/); [Meta help](https://www.meta.com/help/superintelligence/2124746764949121/)). **Claude Duet** runs Opus 5.5 with its own browser on a VM and asks before paying, sending, signing or deleting ([MacRumors](https://www.macrumors.com/2026/10/21/anthropic-claude-duet/); [gHacks](https://www.ghacks.net/2026/10/23/anthropic-launches-claude-duet-a-background-ai-agent-for-claude-pro-and-max-subscribers-in-the-us/)). **ChatGPT Dots** buy with a card already saved on the merchant's site after user sign-off, or with pre-approval for one named purchase (secondary source, [usecarly](https://www.usecarly.com/blog/chatgpt-dots/)). **Grok Bot** shows an approval card with merchant and amount, then Link issues a one-time virtual card the bot never sees, and unanswered requests expire after about 10 minutes (secondary; xAI has not published full docs, [DEV](https://dev.to/rohan_techofficial_81a0f/grok-bot-got-a-wallet-what-xais-shopping-agent-actually-does-with-your-money-1n3g)). **Instinct**'s terms appoint the service as the user's agent for binding transactions, but its payment mechanism is unpublished ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/10/05/instinct-brings-its-ai-agent-to-group-chats-even-for-friends-without-an-account/)). **Atomic** is the only local-first peer: it runs llama.cpp on the desktop, hands planning to a cloud model in its opt-in "Fusion" mode, and advertises no purchases and no mobile build ([Atomic docs](https://atomicagent.io/docs/features/fusion/)). Atomic's Fusion is the same architecture as Hermit's Boost.

None of these products publishes task success or latency. The public parity bar comes from benchmarks. The human-judged Online-Mind2Web leaderboard tops out at 97.7% ([Yutori leaderboard](https://yutori.com/leaderboards/online-mind2web)), and OpenAI reported Operator at 87% on WebVoyager (vendor, [OpenAI CUA](https://openai.com/index/computer-using-agent/)). The failures are as instructive as the scores. Comet bought from a fake Walmart store with an autofilled card and no confirmation ([BleepingComputer](https://www.bleepingcomputer.com/news/security/perplexitys-comet-ai-browser-tricked-into-buying-fake-items-online/)). Atlas stopped at the purchase button itself, but Zenity routed the checkout through Amazon's own Rufus assistant ([Zenity Labs](https://labs.zenity.io/post/grand-theft-atlas)). Amazon blocked Muse for not identifying itself ([TechRepublic](https://www.techrepublic.com/article/news-meta-muse-ai-agent-us-launch/)). The demand signal is also weaker than the launches suggest: **OpenAI scaled back in-chat Instant Checkout in March 2026** after roughly 12 to 30 Shopify merchants went live ([Modern Retail](https://www.modernretail.co/technology/what-went-wrong-with-chatgpts-instant-checkout/)).

| Below 2B | None found | | Searches for 0.5B to 0.8B web fine-tunes returned nothing |

The table changes the repo's premise. `docs/research/oss-computer-use.md` concluded from WebRL's 4.8% untuned 8B baseline that on-device navigation is not viable. The same family of results now shows **Fara1.5-4B roughly matching Operator's 58.3% on OM2W**, and Fara1.5 uses Hermit's own Qwen3.5 family, tokenizer and chat template ([Microsoft Research](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/articles/fara1-5-computer-use-agent/)). Fara's scaling slope is shallow (about 15 points of OM2W from 4B to 27B), and 2B Android agents went from 7 to 9% ([pith](https://pith.science/paper/2501.04575)) to 49 to 56% in about a year. Extrapolating below 4B remains speculation. The honest summary is that sub-1B open-web navigation is unproven, not disproven, and the first lab to measure it gets to define the baseline.

The decisive result for Hermit's design is the 4B skill-executor study. **A single 4B agent trained on whole tasks barely moved (38.7% to 39.2%), while a planner plus a trained skill executor moved 12.9 points, and single-primitive success rose from 55.3% to 80.3%** ([alphaXiv 2610.35823](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2610.skill-centric-generalist-web-agents), preprint). Small models fail at composing long horizons, not at choosing a single step. Composition is exactly what Hermit already moves into code.

## Five structured paths already run on the phone

The cheapest web step is the one that never touches a page. CMU's Beyond Browsing measured this on WebArena with GPT-4o: **browsing alone scored 14.8%, APIs alone 29.2%, and a hybrid 38.9%** ([arXiv 2410.16464](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.16464v3)). SkillWeaver turned explored sites into executable API functions and lifted four live sites from 40.2 to 56.2 (secondary summary, [GitHub](https://github.com/jjakimoto/research-issues/issues/1418)). Agent Skill Induction kept only programmatic skills that passed on execution and beat the static baseline by 23.5% with 10.7 to 15.3% fewer steps ([arXiv 2504.06821](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.06821)). The phone's first stage is therefore a menu of typed paths, with the 0.8B model acting as router and slot filler.

### Search and lookups where code does the crawling

The 2026 "Search, Do not Guess" paper is the most relevant small-model result. **Models under 4B call search less often and hallucinate more, and an always-search policy took Qwen3-0.6B from 19.4 to 47.0 String-F1 on HotpotQA**. A 1.7B student given the teacher's evidence jumped to 74.7%, which the authors read as proof that retrieval, not reasoning, is the bottleneck ([arXiv 2604.04651](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2604.04651)). Letting small models skip search on their most confident queries cost 4.8 to 15.0 points, against 0.3 to 0.6 for a 32B model. Hermit should therefore force at least one search on every web-intent turn rather than let BotPsy decide. DGPO shows the training path below 1B: a Qwen2.5-0.5B student reached 44.6 EM on HotpotQA against 43.5 for its 3B teacher ([arXiv 2508.20324](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2508.20324v2)). Hard browsing stays out of reach, since even the always-search 1.7B scores about 10 on BrowseComp-Plus and 25 on Frames. On-phone "research" should be scoped to lookups and comparisons, not puzzles.

The pipeline that makes this work runs almost entirely in code. Code rewrites the query with templates, fans out searches in parallel, reranks snippets, fetches two to four pages in parallel, and parses JSON-LD first. **JSON-LD is used by 70% of sites that annotate structured data** ([WDC 2024](https://www.uni-mannheim.de/dws/news/wdc-json-ld-microdata-rdfa-data-corpus-2024-published)), and Product/Offer and LocalBusiness fields answer price, availability, hours and address with no reader pass at all. Only when no structured field exists does the quarantined reader see the top two or three chunks, and it returns typed `{claim, value, quote, url}` records. Price comparison should never ask a 0.8B model to compare numbers: code normalizes currency and sorts, and the model writes one sentence. This is how Perplexity Shopping works at scale, with merchant feeds plus crawled product pages carrying structured price and seller fields ([Productsup](https://www.productsup.com/blog/what-is-perplexity-ai-shopping-key-features-pricing-and-use-cases/)). Qwen3-Reranker-0.6B is the strongest small multilingual reranker found, at **MMTEB-R 66.36 against 58.36 for bge-reranker-v2-m3** at the same size, but its GGUF path has known llama.cpp breakage and its phone latency is unmeasured ([HF card](https://huggingface.co/Qwen/Qwen3-Reranker-0.6B); [ggml-org Q8_0](https://huggingface.co/ggml-org/Qwen3-Reranker-0.6B-Q8_0-GGUF)). For extraction, Kotlin ports of Mozilla Readability exist ([Readability4J](https://github.com/dankito/Readability4J)). ReaderLM-v2 is CC-BY-NC and larger than BotPsy, so it is out ([HF](https://huggingface.co/jinaai/ReaderLM-v2)). The search provider is still OQ-04. Brave is the best-documented privacy-oriented API at about $5 per 1,000 requests, but its pricing sources conflict and are secondary ([ecorpit](https://ecorpit.com/agent-web-search-api-cost-comparison-2026/)). An API key embedded in the APK can be extracted, which pushes toward the opt-in relay pattern Q24 already approved.

### Commerce through typed connectors, not pages

**Shopify Shop MCP (shipped Oct 13, 2026) is the single best purchase path for a small model.** It has six tools (`search_catalog`, `lookup_catalog`, `get_product`, `complete_checkout`, `get_checkout`, `search_orders`) and credential-free search over more than a billion products. Sign-in with Shop works through a device flow, and `complete_checkout` is called only after the shopper confirms total and payment method ([Shopify changelog](https://shopify.dev/changelog/add-shopping-and-shop-pay-checkout-to-any-agent-with-shop-mcp)). That maps one to one onto Hermit's approval sheet. Instacart has an official OAuth MCP server ([ChatForest](https://chatforest.com/guides/mcp-food-restaurant/), secondary). Uber's hosted endpoint is described by directories but was not verified on an Uber page ([mcp.so](https://mcp.so/servers/uber), unverified). DoorDash, Uber Eats, Grubhub, OpenTable, Expedia and Booking.com have no confirmed official public MCP servers. Shop MCP pays through Shop Pay, which is a card rail. Merchant eligibility and rate limits for unregistered agents are not documented.

**x402 with Tether WDK is the only permissionless rail where Hermit's self-custodial wallet pays directly.** WDK's docs describe its wallets as drop-in x402 signers on EVM chains where USD₮0 is deployed ([WDK x402](https://docs.wdk.tether.io/ai/x402)), which fits ADR-015's `packages/payments` design. Its merchants are APIs, data, pages and MCP tools, not retail goods. Cloudflare's x402 Monetization Gateway is still a waitlist ([InfoQ](https://infoq.com/news/2026/07/cloudflare-aws-x402-micropayment/)), Stripe's x402 settles USDC on Base, Solana and Tempo ([The Block](https://www.theblock.co/post/389352/stripe-adds-x402-integration-usdc-agent-payments)), and USDT on x402 is an announcement ([PR Newswire](http://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/utexo-and-x402-enable-usdt-payments-for-the-agent-economy-with-near-instant-settlement-302745083.html)). Asset and chain mismatch is the practical blocker. No shipped protocol found lets Hermit buy at an ordinary store with USDT. Card-network agent rails (Visa Intelligent Commerce, Mastercard Agent Pay, UCP direct checkout) all require agent registration and Web Bot Auth keys ([Cloudflare](https://blog.cloudflare.com/secure-agentic-commerce); [Visa Developer](https://developer.visa.com/capabilities/visa-intelligent-commerce/overview)). For Hermit they are a business-development track, not an engineering one.

### WebMCP capture is cheap to build and covers almost nothing yet

WebMCP is ideal input for a small model: typed tools with JSON schemas, registered by the site itself. It is a W3C Community Group draft live only as a Chrome origin trial from Chrome 149 ([Chrome for Developers](https://developer.chrome.com/blog/ai-webmcp-origin-trial)). The Blink intent lists Android WebView as a trial platform with a shipping target of 157 ([blink-dev](https://groups.google.com/a/chromium.org/g/blink-dev/c/gmYffo5WOE8/m/OJxuQRP3AAAJ)). **Production adoption was "approximately zero" in July 2026**, even among named trial participants such as Shopify, Instacart, Expedia and Target ([Spronta](https://www.spronta.com/blog/state-of-webmcp-july-2026/)). Hermit can wrap `registerTool` from `injectedJavaScriptBeforeContentLoaded`, capture each descriptor and callback, and expose them as typed skills through the bridge, polyfill style, whether or not the WebView ships the API. This is an untested inference. It is worth building as a small shim that logs every registration it sees, so Hermit learns adoption from real browsing rather than from trackers. Shopify's browser-side WebMCP checkout tools also require Web Bot Auth with registered keys ([shopify.dev](https://shopify.dev/docs/agents/checkout)).

### Recipes turn the model into a repair tool

FR-WEB-10 already specifies recipes: a completed, approved task is saved as role-and-name steps with the user's values as variables, replayed without the model, and the model is called only for a failing step. The published evidence for workflow memory is large: AWM reports 24.6% and 51.1% relative gains on Mind2Web and WebArena ([arXiv 2409.07429](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2409.07429)), and ASI's verified program skills beat text memory by 11.3% ([arXiv 2504.06821](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.06821)). For a phone, the important property is not the accuracy gain but the cost profile: a replay is network-bound and model-free, so "reorder my usual" can be faster on the phone than on any cloud agent.

|---|---|---|---|
| A. Factual lookup (hours, address, a fact) | Forced search, JSON-LD first, quarantined reader | None needed | Phone |
| B. Compare prices or products | Code extracts and sorts offers; model phrases | Pages that defeat extraction | Phone with Boost spill |
| C. Buy through a connector | Shop MCP, Instacart, Uber (if verified) | None needed | Phone |
| D. Pay per use | x402 via WDK under caps | None | Phone (ADR-015) |
| E. Known flow on a known site | FR-WEB-05 to 10, recipes, 15 steps | Recipe misses the model cannot repair | Phone |
| F. Known flow on an unseen site, under 10 steps | Not yet | Runs here | Gate to phone |
| G. Open-ended, multi-site, long horizon | Not yet | Runs here | Last to move |
| H. Read behind my logins | WebView with vault, exact origin | None | Phone (US-WEB-06) |

## Boost holds the long tail until measured gates move each family

PDR-026 already states the rule: a family moves to the phone when its on-phone eval passes the gate Boost passes. The research supplies what the gate must contain, because aggregate accuracy hides the failures users feel. **pass^k turns a 90% pass^1 agent into about 43% at k=8** with independent failures ([aiwiki tau2](https://www.aiwiki.ai/wiki/tau2-bench), secondary). Benchmarks with agentic judges inflate scores, and Browser Use's 97 to 98% self-reported OM2W number is not comparable to the human-judged board ([Browser Use](https://browser-use.com/posts/online-mind2web-benchmark)). The proposed gate has four parts, and the thresholds are proposals for the product owner, not findings. First, non-inferiority: on a frozen per-family case set run identically on the phone and on Boost, phone pass^4 within a fixed margin of Boost's (for example 5 points). Second, safety: zero `spend`, `credential` or `send` violations on an adaptive injection set and a data-injection set (fake prices, look-alike domains, swapped addresses). Third, latency: P90 time-to-approval-sheet under a family bar on the S26, with thermal state logged. Fourth, freshness: a blind holdout written after the harness freeze, plus a side-by-side human preference check against Boost.

### Stages, and what each one has to prove

| Stage | New on the phone | Model's job | Gate to reach it |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0 (V1) | Search, reader, connectors, x402, known flows, recipes | Route, fill slots, repair one recipe step | Existing V1 acceptance plus injection set (NFR-07) |
| 1 | Code-first lookup and comparison as default for A and B | Phrase the answer | Family A and B gates vs Boost; structured-hit rate logged |
| 2 | Dedicated web-step model on recipe misses and known kinds on unseen sites | Pick 1 of K pre-filtered candidates, or type a value into ref N | Offline step accuracy on Mind2Web-style sets, then family E and F gates |
| 3 | Planner plus skill executor for short unseen-site tasks, with short retries | Executor picks within one primitive; code or a tiny planner sequences primitives | Family F gate at pass^4; adaptive red team |
| 4 | Open-ended multi-site tasks | Planner over skills, long horizon | Family G gate; only after a published sub-1B result replicates |

Stage 2 is where a dedicated sub-1B model earns its place, and the recipe is now well specified. A frontier teacher works over Hermit's exact accessibility-tree format. MolmoWeb shows an AX-tree teacher transfers, and its open mix includes about 70K AX-tree teacher trajectories ([arXiv 2604.08516](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.08516v1)). A judge filters the trajectories: Agent-as-Annotators reached **41.5% on WebArena with a 9B student trained on 2,322 filtered trajectories** ([arXiv 2604.07776](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.07776)). Training uses skill-level segments rather than whole tasks, since 3,128 skill trajectories on 52 synthetic sites were enough for a 4B executor ([alphaXiv 2610.35823](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2610.skill-centric-generalist-web-agents)). Checkout flows train on synthetic site replicas, which Fara1.5 credits with lifting WebVoyager domains from 73.4 to 83.4 ([Microsoft Research](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/articles/fara1-5-computer-use-agent/)). Fara1.5 used no RL at all, so SFT plus judge filtering is the first rung. DGPO-style teacher-guided RL, built for sub-1B students that get no reward signal from plain RL, is the second ([arXiv 2508.20324](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2508.20324v2)).

Stage 3 borrows the strongest test-time result for small web agents: **three parallel 30-step runs reached 86.2% on WebVoyager for MolmoWeb-8B against 78.2% for one 100-step run, and pass@4 lifted OM2W from 35.3 to 60.5** ([arXiv 2604.08516](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.08516v1)). On a phone, "parallel" applies to the browser and the network, not the model. Hermit can keep several WebViews loading at once, but prefill is compute-bound, so retries must be short and sequential, and the selector has to be code (end-state checks against the task's expected outcome) rather than a second model. Web-Shepherd's process reward model gains 10.9 points with best-of-n ([arXiv 2505.15277](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.15277)), but per-step search multiplies phone latency, so it belongs on Boost first. WebDreamer's world-model planning gained little with a 7B world model ([arXiv 2411.06559](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2411.06559)) and should not be attempted on the phone.

### A PRD conflict blocks Boost from doing this job

US-WEB-10 sends open-ended browsing to Boost, but US-BOOST-03 says **"vault, wallet and tainted turns never route to Boost"** (`docs/prd/hermit-prd.md`). An open-ended web task is tainted from its first page load (FR-CAGE-04), so as written Boost can never run the family it exists for. The consistent reading of ADR-010 ("the cage stays on the phone") is that Boost may read untrusted pages and propose actions, while authority (approvals, vault, wallet, signed log) never leaves the phone. That needs an explicit decision before any V2 Boost web work starts.

## Safety holds only where code, not the model, decides

Frontier vendors report injection rates near zero for their own models. Anthropic reports Opus 5 with Auto Mode at 0% across 129 browser scenarios and 3.7% without the extra layers (vendor, [The Decoder](https://the-decoder.com/opus-5-may-have-solved-browser-based-prompt-injection-the-biggest-security-flaw-haunting-ai-agents/)). Those figures depend on frontier judgement and do not transfer to a 0.8B model. The defenses that do transfer are architectural, and Hermit already has the skeleton in ADR-005's cage and FR-WEB-03's quarantined reader.

**CaMeL** separates a privileged planner, which sees only the trusted user query, from a quarantined model that parses untrusted data but cannot call tools. An interpreter tracks provenance and enforces policy at every call. With o3 it solved 77% of AgentDojo tasks with provable security, against 84% undefended, at about 2.82 times the input tokens ([arXiv 2503.18813](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.18813)). Its utility with a sub-1B planner has never been measured, and that is a publishable experiment in itself. **Progent**'s deterministic allow and deny policies cut AgentDojo injection success from 39.9% to 1.0%, and from 25.8% to 4.2% on a weak open-weight model, with an adaptive attack failing to raise it ([arXiv 2504.11703](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.11703); [arXiv 2607.05120](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.05120)). The same independent work found the limit: **under "agent data injection," which manipulates values rather than instructions, GPT-5.2 with Progent still fell for 22.2% of attacks** ([arXiv 2607.05120](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.05120)), because a policy cannot tell a malicious price or address from a real one. The adaptive-evaluation paper warns that every such defense was validated mainly on static benchmarks ([arXiv 2606.26479](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.26479)).

The web cage for Hermit therefore has six layers, each enforced in code. The first is planner and reader separation: the reader returns typed values (price as decimal, domain as string, sku) that can never become instructions, which is exactly the mixing Brave found at the root of Comet's failures ([PromptLayer](https://blog.promptlayer.com/browser-agent-security-risk/)). The second is a Progent-style policy file at every `remote_write`, `spend` and `credential` call. The origin must equal the vault entry's exact origin, the merchant must be on the task's domain list, the amount must be within PDR-011's caps, and the ship-to address must be one saved in the vault. The third is FIDES-style taint on arguments: any value derived from quarantined content is marked on the approval sheet ([arXiv 2505.23643](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.23643)). The fourth is FR-WEB-07's deterministic commit classifier and FR-WEB-09's re-validation after approval, both ported from Nanobrowser and already specified. The fifth closes the Atlas hole: during checkout, the WebView blocks or ignores on-site chat assistants so a second agent cannot commit for the first. The sixth is redaction of receipts and confirmations before anything is shared, because the Grok demo leaked two addresses and an order number through its output ([Ynet](https://www.ynetnews.com/tech-and-digital/article/b1bfjwksfe)).

### Money: scoped credentials, and no chargeback on stablecoins

The industry pattern is that **the agent never holds raw card data**. It gets a scoped credential minted per purchase after approval: a Link one-time card, a Stripe Shared Payment Token, a Visa or Mastercard agent token, or a Stripe Issuing card restricted by MCC and amount ([Crossmint](https://www.crossmint.com/learn/agent-card-payments-compared), secondary). Hermit has no backend by design (ADR-009), so it cannot issue cards itself. Near term, card purchases go through Shop Pay inside Shop MCP, where Shopify holds the card, or through the user's own saved merchant card behind the approval sheet. Single-use cards need an issuer partner, which is a business decision. **Stablecoin payments through x402 have no chargeback and no issuer-side reversal.** An injection that reaches `spend` is final. The wallet that pays agents should therefore be a separate small-balance account with hard per-payment and daily caps, biometric signing per transaction, an approval that expires (Grok's 10 minutes is a reasonable reference), and a payee allowlist. The signed hash-chained log becomes Hermit's dispute evidence on card rails and its only evidence on stablecoin rails. Running visibly and identifiably also matters for access. Unregistered agents are treated as bots on Cloudflare-protected sites ([PPC Land](https://ppc.land/agent-identity/)), so publishing a Web Bot Auth key directory is cheap insurance.

## Experiments that would make Hermit a reference lab

Five gaps in the literature line up with Hermit's position. No sub-1B web navigation result exists. No defense has been evaluated with a sub-1B planner. No Spanish or LatAm benchmark exists for small search agents. No on-device end-to-end latency for a small web agent has been published. No one has tested WebMCP capture in an embedded WebView. Each experiment below should be pre-registered (cases frozen and hashed before the first run), reported at pass^k with seeds, and published with the harness, the case sets and the model weights where licenses allow.

**E1, sub-1B element selection.** Measure offline step accuracy on Mind2Web-style element selection for Qwen3.5-0.8B base, BotPsy, and a fine-tuned web-step model. Run it twice, once over the full pruned tree and once over K code-prefiltered candidates. This is cheap, runs on the Mac with the phone's GGUF, and answers the question every later stage depends on: how often a 0.8B picks the right ref.

**E2, the first published sub-1B web navigation result.** Train a Qwen3.5-0.8B web-step executor on skill-level segments (MiniWebGen-style), with a code or small-model planner. Evaluate on WebArena-Lite, an OM2W subset and held-out synthetic sites. Report the same recipe at 0.8B, 2B and 4B to draw the first size curve below 4B. A negative result is also publishable and still sets the baseline.

**E3, always-search in Spanish.** Replicate the always-search finding at 0.8B on a new ES-LatAm lookup set (store hours, prices, government procedures), with and without JSON-LD-first extraction. Report the structured-hit rate by country, which also answers the open question of LatAm structured-data coverage.

**E4, CaMeL with a tiny planner.** Measure AgentDojo-style utility and attack success with BotPsy as the privileged planner and a quarantined reader, under static, adaptive and data-injection attacks.

**E5, phone latency of a real web agent.** Publish S26 numbers for every family: time to first source, time to approval sheet, prefill tokens per step, cache hit rate and thermal state.

**E6, WebMCP in a WebView.** Show whether wrapping `registerTool` before page load exposes site tools to an embedded agent, and log real-world registration counts across Hermit's browsing.

**E7, short retries on a phone.** Compare one long run against several short runs under an equal on-phone prefill budget, with code-only end-state selection.

**E8, harness ablation per family.** At every model upgrade, run the family suite with each harness layer removed, so rules the model has absorbed can be deleted.

Training cost is modest: a bf16 LoRA on Qwen3.5-0.8B needs about 3 GB of VRAM ([Unsloth docs](https://unsloth.ai/docs/fr/modeles/qwen3.5/fine-tune)). The dev Mac cannot train, and teacher inference dominates the budget. Open data from MolmoWeb, Go-Browse ([GitHub](https://github.com/ApGa/Go-Browse)) and Web-Shepherd ([GitHub](https://github.com/kyle8581/Web-Shepherd)) needs conversion to Hermit's tree format and a license check before use.

## First-principles thinking: a web task is a product of steps and a sum of prefills

A web task's success is the product of its per-step success rates. At 95% per step, a 15-step task succeeds 46% of the time (0.95^15), a 5-step task 77%, and a 2-call connector task about 90%. Raising per-step accuracy to 99% brings the 15-step task to 86%. The levers, in order, are therefore: remove steps (connectors, recipes, structured data), make each remaining step a short multiple choice (prefiltered candidates, grammar-pinned refs), and only then make the model smarter. Every published small-model jump fits this order. APIs beat browsing, skills beat whole tasks, and planners beat end-to-end agents.

A task's latency is the sum over steps of new prompt tokens divided by prefill speed, plus generated tokens divided by decode speed. At Hermit's roughly 200 tok/s prefill, a 3,000-character tree is roughly 800 tokens, so even with a perfectly cached static prefix each step costs about 4 s of prefill, and 15 steps about 60 s before any decoding. These are estimates from the arithmetic, not measurements. If code reduces the observation to 10 candidate lines (about 150 tokens), a step drops below 1 s. The tree format in FR-WEB-05 is right for Boost. On the phone, the executor should see the skill's target description plus a candidate list ranked by code. The model's irreducible job is then small: turn the user's words into slots, choose when code cannot, and say when the task is infeasible. Fetching, parsing, comparing numbers, sequencing primitives, checking origins and enforcing caps are all deterministic.

The Qwen3.5 hybrid architecture adds one more constraint, carried over from the earlier harness research. Its DeltaNet layers cannot rewind, so any change before the end of the cached prefix forces a full re-prefill. A web loop must build each step's prompt as a byte-stable prefix (instructions and skill schemas) plus a tail (task, last outcome lines, current candidates), never by editing earlier text.

## Contrarian thinking: where the consensus is wrong for Hermit

"Parity means a general browser agent" is the wrong target. The competitors' own behavior argues against it: OpenAI pulled checkout out of chat after a handful of merchants ([Modern Retail](https://www.modernretail.co/technology/what-went-wrong-with-chatgpts-instant-checkout/)), Amazon blocks agents outright, and retail payment runs on Shop Pay and Link rails that reward typed integrations over clicking. Parity is the user getting the outcome. For most retail purchases in late 2026, the fastest correct path is a connector, not a browser.

"Bigger is the only way" is weaker than it looks. Fara1.5-4B roughly matches Operator on OM2W with SFT alone, and 2B Android agents rose about sevenfold in a year. The repo's "not viable on device" line was written from a 2024 baseline. The opposite consensus, "it is only a matter of time," is equally unproven below 2B. That is why the gate, not optimism, should move each family.

"Never pixels" is right for the phone and incomplete for parity. Screenshot models lead the live-web boards, but an AX-tree agent with Gemini-3-flash scored 85.6 on WebVoyager, ahead of every small screenshot model ([arXiv 2604.08516](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.08516v1)). Text is enough for most of the web. Canvas-heavy and custom-widget sites are where Boost, with pixels allowed, keeps a lasting role.

"Boost is the safe fallback" is false as specified, because US-BOOST-03 forbids tainted turns. It is also less private than it sounds once the confidential-cloud variant arrives in V3. Fetching from the phone exposes the user's IP to every site, and browsing from a computer or enclave hides it. The privacy ranking depends on whom the user fears: Hermit, the merchant or the network.

"A small model's weakness is a security risk" is half wrong. WASP found web agents often start following injections but rarely complete the attacker's goal, which its authors call "security by incompetence" ([arXiv 2504.18575](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.18575)). A 0.8B model may be harder to steer to a full exploit today. That protection disappears as the web-step model improves, so every capability gain must ship with a fresh adaptive red-team run.

## Paradoxical thinking: tensions that point to the design

The fastest web agent is the one that browses least. Structured hits answer in a few seconds without a reader pass, and recipe replays need no model at all. A product that uses the model less looks smarter and faster.

Success starves the model of easy cases. Every completed task becomes a recipe, so the web-step model only ever sees recipe misses, redesigned pages and unseen sites, which are the hardest slice of the distribution. Offline benchmarks will overstate its field accuracy. The eval for Stage 2 must be built from recipe-miss traces, not from fresh tasks.

The more capable the web model becomes, the less it should be allowed to decide. Capability raises both utility and attack success, so authority must stay in code as competence grows. CaMeL's 77% against 84% is the price of that separation, and it is worth paying.

The most private rail is the least forgiving. Self-custody is Hermit's identity, yet a self-custodial stablecoin payment has no chargeback, while the card rails that protect consumers require the agent registration and identification a privacy-first client resists. Hermit should be anonymous where it reads and identified where it pays.

The goal that defines Hermit is the one it should not ship yet. PDR-026 makes on-phone open web tasks Hermit's distinctive long-term bet. The way to honor it is to publish the measurements, including the failures, and to move families only when gates pass. A research lab is defined by its ruler, not by its claims.

## Conclusion

The question has changed shape. A single model that does everything on the phone is not the bar. The bar is a phone that owns more task families with each passing gate, while Boost handles a shrinking remainder. Families A through E and H can run on the phone with V1 components plus a code-first search pipeline, because their difficulty lives in plumbing, not reasoning. Family F is the research frontier, and the 4B skill-executor result suggests it can reach a sub-1B model only through decomposition: a planner in code or a tiny model, an executor that picks among a handful of candidates, and short retries with code-checked end states. Family G stays on Boost until a sub-1B navigation result exists and replicates. Hermit is positioned to produce that result, because it already owns the harness, the eval lab and the phone.

Two findings should change the plan now. Boost, as specified, cannot run tainted web tasks, so the boundary between "may read" and "may act" needs a decision before V2 starts. And payment safety for a self-custodial agent depends less on the model than on the wallet's shape: small balances, hard caps, per-transaction biometrics and expiring approvals, because nothing can reverse a mistake on that rail.
