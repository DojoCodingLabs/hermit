# Multi-bot benchmark: how AI assistants run several specialized bots, for Hermit's multi-bot plan

Researched 2026-11-24 with web search and the vendors' own pages. It feeds the plan for Hermit's multi-bot feature: several bots with their own jobs, schedules and memory, where today Hermit has exactly one chat.

- **What this adds:** `docs/research/design-benchmark.md` (2026-11-17) covered visual design and approvals for the same products. This file reuses its sources and adds one dimension: what a bot is, how several bots are listed, scheduled and coordinated, and how they are kept safe from each other.
- **Markers:** (official) means a vendor site, help center, docs, release notes or store listing. (secondary) means press, a review, a forum post, a third-party guide or social media. (unverified) means we could not confirm it. A page we could not read is named as blocked.
- **Scope rule:** searches used product and feature names only.
- **The question:** how do assistants run several specialized bots? For each product: what a bot is, how bots are listed and pinned, how they run on a schedule, who coordinates them, whether they message each other, and what keeps them apart.

## 1. What we found, in one paragraph

Few products run several bots. Grok Bot is the clearest case: a messenger of bots, one designated coordinator (the starred "primary Bot", which xAI's launch post called a chief of staff), routines per bot, and bots that message each other asynchronously. Perplexity Teammates (launched 2026-11-04), Cue by Manus and the self-hosted OpenClaw ship similar rosters. dots, Claude Duet and Muse do not: each is one agent that runs its helpers out of sight, and its channels or side chats are topics, not bots. OpenAI and Google are even retiring their persona products (GPTs, Gems) in favor of one assistant with skills. Where several bots exist, the same answers recur: a coordinator as the front door, memory per bot with a small shared profile, schedules in plain words with a run history, handoffs shown as folded events, and approvals that expire when nobody started the work. So do the weak spots: Grok Bot's bots share one computer, its logins and the saved card; nothing in code stops its bots from messaging each other; and an hourly schedule can burn a week of usage in a day. Everything above runs in the cloud. On a phone with one 0.8B model, Hermit can copy the shape (a roster, a coordinator, per-bot memory and schedules), but its bots must take turns on one model, wake when Android allows (hourly is realistic on the charger, not on battery), do one small step per wake, and hand long research to Boost.

## 3. Grok Bot in short, and who else runs several bots

### 3.1 What Grok Bot documents

Links in this table are official unless marked secondary.

| Feature | What the sources say | In short |
|---|---|---|
| Bots and chats | A Bot has "its own conversation" ([docs: bots](https://docs.x.ai/grok-bot/bots)), and "the main objects in Grok Bot are Bots, not conversations" ([xAI: Designing Grok Bot](https://x.ai/news/designing-grok-bot)). Fresh chats with the same Bot ([Cursor forum](https://forum.cursor.com/t/grok-bot-ultra-users-how-do-you-make-the-weekly-allowance-last-mine-reached-99-in-three-days/171221), secondary), threads, and group chats of two to six Bots ([docs: chat](https://docs.x.ai/grok-bot/chat-and-collaboration)) also exist | One main chat per bot, with exceptions |
| Routines | A routine runs on a schedule asked for in plain words, such as "every 2 hours"; schedules "must be at least five minutes apart"; each run follows the routine's saved **Instruction** and posts in the Bot's chat ([docs: skills and routines](https://docs.x.ai/grok-bot/skills-routines-and-automations)). The user or a Bot sets it up; it is not a built-in heartbeat. The docs warn that "An hourly schedule ... can use a week of usage in a day" | Schedules per bot, with a cost warning |
| Roles | Any role is set in the first message ([docs: bots](https://docs.x.ai/grok-bot/bots)); the launch page showed eight starter roles ([eesel](https://www.eesel.ai/blog/grok-bot), secondary; listed in 4.1) | A role is text, not a type |
| Pins and the star | "**Pin** active Bots to keep them at the top of the sidebar", with no limit stated ([docs: bots](https://docs.x.ai/grok-bot/bots)). The coordinator is "marked with a star in the sidebar" ([docs: initiative](https://docs.x.ai/grok-bot/initiative)), which is separate from pinning | Pins with no documented limit; the coordinator is starred, not pinned |
| The coordinator | The "primary Bot", one per account, "checks in with you on its own and coordinates work with your other Bots" ([docs: bots](https://docs.x.ai/grok-bot/bots)). "Chief of staff" is xAI's launch wording ([xAI: launch](https://x.ai/news/introducing-grok-bot)) and a common template name, not a separate type. It creates Bots, hands off work and reports for them; it has no documented power to edit their memory, rules or routines | One starred coordinator per account |
| Messages between bots | "A Bot can send an asynchronous message to another Bot. The receiving Bot wakes, handles the request, and can reply later." There is "no switch" to stop it, and "Every Bot reply counts toward your usage, including ... messages between Bots" ([docs: chat](https://docs.x.ai/grok-bot/chat-and-collaboration)) | Direct and asynchronous, with no off switch |
| Isolation | Roles, memory and routines are separate, but every Bot uses one cloud computer with shared files, browser sign-ins and Vault cards: "Do not use separate Bots as a security boundary" ([docs: FAQ](https://docs.x.ai/grok-bot/faq)) | Separate roles, shared access |

### 3.2 Which other products run several bots

As of 2026-11-24, three other products do, and dots, Claude Duet and Muse each run one agent:

- **dots** has one dot per person. OpenAI: "Over time, we envision teams of dots working together on your behalf" and "In the future, you'll be able to add more dots" (4.2).
- **Claude Duet** is one agent. Its channels are topics that "share the same memory", not separate bots (4.3).
- **Muse** is one agent with side chats. Its sub-agents are hidden, and a second agent, Sentinel, only grants permissions (4.4).
- **Similar to Grok Bot:** Perplexity Teammates (4.8), Cue by Manus (4.7) and the self-hosted OpenClaw (4.5) each run several named agents that hand work to each other. OpenClaw even ships a "chief of staff" coordinator preset.
- **What the single-agent products share with Grok Bot** is the coordinator idea turned inward: one front door that splits work among helpers the user doesn't manage (dots' background agents, Muse's sub-agents, Manus's Wide Research, Atomic Agent's Fusion workers).

## 4. Per-product findings

### 4.1 Grok Bot (xAI with Cursor)

The deepest section. Sources are the product docs at docs.x.ai (read in full as Markdown on 2026-11-24), three xAI posts (launch on 2026-08-11, "Designing Grok Bot" on 2026-09-03, "Grok Bot 1.0" on 2026-11-11), the App Store listing, Cursor's forum and press. Grok Bot is a separate app from the Grok chat app and signs in with a Cursor account; the docs now name the company SpaceXSI ([docs: FAQ](https://docs.x.ai/grok-bot/faq)).

**What a Bot is**
- "A Bot is a durable AI teammate with a name, a job, its own conversation, and working context that develops over time." ([docs: bots](https://docs.x.ai/grok-bot/bots), official)
- **Creating one:** **New Bot** (+, or Cmd/Ctrl+N), then a name, a shape and a color, then **Create**; the chat opens and the first message should give "its job, sources, and approval boundaries, and ask it to remember them" ([docs: bots](https://docs.x.ai/grok-bot/bots), official). There is no form for the role: "Personal Bots no longer have a description or a **Bot settings** page; to change what a Bot does, tell it in chat." ([docs: bots](https://docs.x.ai/grok-bot/bots), official). At launch a Bot still had a description field ("A 'chief of staff' is just a role you write into a description field"), and the product page showed eight roles: Sales Outbound, Talent Scout, Paid Media, Expense Manager, Product Performance, Bug Reproduction, Account Health, Chief of Staff ([eesel, 2026-08-12](https://www.eesel.ai/blog/grok-bot), secondary). The field has since gone; the role now lives in the chat and the Bot's memory.
- **Other ways to get a Bot:** existing Bots "can also suggest or create a focused Bot when a job should have a long-lived owner"; templates from a public gallery (**Import Bot**) or a shared link (**Share as Template**, which exposes "its identity, description, skills, and routines" but never the computer, logins or history); **Duplicate** on iPhone copies the profile, skills, routines and avatar but "not conversation history, learned memory, or attachments" ([docs: bots](https://docs.x.ai/grok-bot/bots), official). The docs recommend a user-made gallery Bot, dr eggbot, that "Asks you a few questions, then sets up the Bots for you, one job each" ([docs: how it works](https://docs.x.ai/grok-bot/how-grok-bot-works), official); its gallery page says it creates them with a `CreateAgent` tool ([x.ai gallery](https://x.ai/bot/marketplace/bots/dr-eggbot-v2), secondary, user-made).
- **Look:** a character built from a shape and color, or a generated or uploaded picture (under 25 MB); a label under the name ([docs: bots](https://docs.x.ai/grok-bot/bots), official). The **Accent** setting colors each 1:1 chat with that Bot's color ([docs: settings](https://docs.x.ai/grok-bot/settings-and-notifications), official). Avatars share "simple shapes and expressive eyes", and their motion shows state: idle, thinking, working, waiting, blocked, done ([xAI: Designing Grok Bot](https://x.ai/news/designing-grok-bot), official).
- **Tools and knowledge are mostly account-wide:** "Connect your mail, calendar, chat, and docs, and every Bot you run can read and work in them" ([docs: use cases](https://docs.x.ai/grok-bot/use-cases), official). "Private skills are one library shared by all your Bots", enabled for a Bot when it can't find one ([docs: skills and routines](https://docs.x.ai/grok-bot/skills-routines-and-automations), official). There is no model picker; Grok Bot picks a model per task, sometimes a third-party one ([docs: FAQ](https://docs.x.ai/grok-bot/faq), official).
- **Limits:** "roughly 50 Bots per account and six per group chat" ([xAI: Designing Grok Bot](https://x.ai/news/designing-grok-bot), official); up to 50 routines per Bot ([docs: skills and routines](https://docs.x.ai/grok-bot/skills-routines-and-automations), official). A third-party guide reports 50 as a combined pool of Bots and group chats ([Continuum](https://continuumcode.ai/guides/grok-bot-limits/), secondary, itself citing an unconfirmed report).

**Chats**
- **One main conversation per Bot.** "The main objects in Grok Bot are Bots, not conversations" ([xAI: Designing Grok Bot](https://x.ai/news/designing-grok-bot), official). Inside it, threads hold feedback on one result or one approval ([docs: chat](https://docs.x.ai/grok-bot/chat-and-collaboration), official). **Reset this conversation?** restarts the history and keeps memory, files, routines and settings ([docs: bots](https://docs.x.ai/grok-bot/bots), official).
- **But not strictly one chat per Bot:** on the forum, users learned they can start a fresh conversation with the same Bot from the + button, and were told to do it per task because long chats cost more ("I did not expect you could have multi chats with same Bot") ([Cursor forum, 2026-09-10](https://forum.cursor.com/t/grok-bot-ultra-users-how-do-you-make-the-weekly-allowance-last-mine-reached-99-in-three-days/171221), secondary).
- **Group chats:** "Use a group when several Bots need one shared outcome and visible handoffs." **New Group Chat**, two to six Bots in **To:**; write normally "to let the participating Bots decide who should respond", or direct with `@` a Bot, **Reply**, or `@everyone` "sparingly" ([docs: chat](https://docs.x.ai/grok-bot/chat-and-collaboration), official). Approval requests, secret and login requests, and drafts never appear in a group; Bot-to-group handoffs are text only. Voice chat is one Bot at a time, never in a group.
- **Other surfaces:** the same Bots appear in X Chat (rolling out) and, for Team Bots, in Slack and Microsoft Teams ([docs: chat](https://docs.x.ai/grok-bot/chat-and-collaboration), [docs: Team Bots](https://docs.x.ai/grok-bot/team-bots), official).

**Home and list**
- **A roster, like a messenger.** The sidebar lists Bots, each with a status preview such as "Need your yes on the Friday all-hands deck", built to be "scanned quickly" as it grows ([xAI: Designing Grok Bot](https://x.ai/news/designing-grok-bot), official). It marks **Needs attention** (a question, approval or handoff), **Unread activity** (a new result) and working or typing status, with a dock badge and manual read/unread ([docs: settings](https://docs.x.ai/grok-bot/settings-and-notifications), official).
- **The primary Bot is starred:** "Your primary Bot, marked with a star in the sidebar." ([docs: initiative](https://docs.x.ai/grok-bot/initiative), official)
- **Pinning:** "**Pin** active Bots to keep them at the top of the sidebar." No limit is documented ([docs: bots](https://docs.x.ai/grok-bot/bots), official); the phone app can "pin or hide a conversation" ([docs: mobile](https://docs.x.ai/grok-bot/mobile), official).
- **Sections, hiding, search, shortcuts:** sections by project, client or business ("Move to new section", synced between desktop and iOS); **Hide from sidebar** keeps a Bot working ("without deleting its work or pausing its routines") under **Hidden Bots** ([docs: bots](https://docs.x.ai/grok-bot/bots), official). Search Bots with Cmd/Ctrl+Shift+F and jump to sidebar Bots 1 to 9 with Cmd/Ctrl+1–9 ([docs: chat](https://docs.x.ai/grok-bot/chat-and-collaboration), official); the phone searches Messages, Bots, Group Chats, Files and Routines ([docs: mobile](https://docs.x.ai/grok-bot/mobile), official).
- **Delete** erases the Bot's profile, conversations, memory, routines and attachments, "including the shared facts it saved for your other Bots", and its shared template; it can't be undone, and files and sign-ins on the shared computer stay ([docs: bots](https://docs.x.ai/grok-bot/bots), official).
- **Notifications are per Bot:** "Get notified when this Bot finishes or is waiting for you" ([docs: mobile](https://docs.x.ai/grok-bot/mobile), official). Group chats have no such switch.
- Users running a coordinator plus specialists ask for more structure: "One-level sidebar folders exist. That is not enough when each project is a coordinator plus specialists"; "A one-level folder still leaves every specialist as a peer of every other project's lead", and related threads ask for sub-bots nested under a main Bot ([Cursor forum, Aug to Nov 2026](https://forum.cursor.com/t/grok-bot-project-folders-that-hold-a-squad-not-just-a-flat-hide-list/168428), secondary).

**Scheduling**
- **Routines:** "A **routine** tells one Bot when to run a workflow: on a schedule, or when something happens", such as a Slack message, a GitHub, Linear, Sentry or PagerDuty event, an email to the Bot's own address, or a webhook ([docs: skills and routines](https://docs.x.ai/grok-bot/skills-routines-and-automations), official). Schedules are asked for in plain words ("every weekday at 8:00 AM" or "every 2 hours") and shown back under **When to run**; the design post's schedule chips include "Every day at 8:00 AM", "Every Monday at 9:00 AM", "Monthly on the 1st at 8:00 AM" and "Every 30 minutes" ([xAI: Designing Grok Bot](https://x.ai/news/designing-grok-bot), official). "A prompt can start a session, but so can a schedule, an event, or another Bot." "Routine schedules must be at least five minutes apart." A new routine waits for its next time; **Test** runs it now and does real work.
- **Where results land:** in the owning Bot's chat, with **Run history** (Running, Succeeded, Failed with the reason; the 20 most recent runs) and the Bot's notification switch ([docs: skills and routines](https://docs.x.ai/grok-bot/skills-routines-and-automations), [docs: mobile](https://docs.x.ai/grok-bot/mobile), official). Routines "run in the cloud while your laptop is closed".
- **Limits and cost:** all routines together up to 300 runs an hour, each routine up to 200; "Each run spends usage ... An hourly schedule, a short interval, or a Slack trigger on a busy channel can use a week of usage in a day." "Grok Bot may ask whether to keep routines running after a long period away and pause them if there is no response." ([docs: skills and routines](https://docs.x.ai/grok-bot/skills-routines-and-automations), official). A forum reply to a user who burned a week's allowance in three days: "A routine every few minutes fires ~100x/day. Prefer hourly or a few times a day, and run recurring jobs on their own dedicated bot (not your main chat)." ([Cursor forum](https://forum.cursor.com/t/grok-bot-ultra-users-how-do-you-make-the-weekly-allowance-last-mine-reached-99-in-three-days/171221), secondary).
- **Stopping:** a Pause/Resume switch per routine (**Active** on the phone), delete per routine, and deleting a Bot removes its routines; hiding does not pause ([docs: skills and routines](https://docs.x.ai/grok-bot/skills-routines-and-automations), official).
- **Check-ins without a schedule:** the primary Bot "Offers to take on work", "Flags problems with your apps" (for example a Gmail sign-out) and follows up. "It offers first." "Notifications control alerts, not the check-ins themselves. If you'd like your Bot's check-ins turned off, contact support." ([docs: initiative](https://docs.x.ai/grok-bot/initiative), official). Grok Bot 1.0 says Bots "can recognize work that needs to be done and offer to take it on" ([xAI: Grok Bot 1.0, 2026-11-11](https://x.ai/news/grok-bot-1-0), official). There is no documented heartbeat interval.
- **Usage model:** a weekly pool shared with Cursor and Grok on Premium, Super and Ultra of The X Subscription (Plus has no Grok Bot) ([docs: X Subscription](https://docs.x.ai/x-subscription/grok-bot), official); a usage chart **By Bot** ([docs: settings](https://docs.x.ai/grok-bot/settings-and-notifications), official). Usage is billed for "tools, browser use, subagents, and re-reading the conversation each turn", not per message ([Cursor forum](https://forum.cursor.com/t/grok-bot-ultra-users-how-do-you-make-the-weekly-allowance-last-mine-reached-99-in-three-days/171221), secondary).

**Coordination**
- **The primary Bot is the coordinator:** "Your primary Bot is your main point of contact. It checks in with you on its own and coordinates work with your other Bots." One per account, chosen at **Introducing your primary Bot** and changed with **Replace with different Bot**; a Team Bot can't be primary ([docs: bots](https://docs.x.ai/grok-bot/bots), official). "Ask your main Bot what's new, and it answers for every Bot working for you." ([docs: overview](https://docs.x.ai/grok-bot), official). The docs advise starting new work there and asking it to create a Bot "when the same kind of request keeps coming back with its own sources, rules, or schedule" ([docs: how it works](https://docs.x.ai/grok-bot/how-grok-bot-works), official), and to "Ask your primary Bot to set up a few Bots that each own one part" (campaign team, month-end close, support queue, each on a weekly or monthly routine) ([docs: use cases](https://docs.x.ai/grok-bot/use-cases), official).
- **"Chief of Staff" is xAI's launch wording, not a separate type:** "A chief of staff sits on top, with a specialist for each lane" ([xAI: launch](https://x.ai/news/introducing-grok-bot), official); the store listing says "Stand up a specialist AI coworker for each lane, then promote one to coordinate when you need a single update" ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/grok-bot/id6794501026), official). The design post says some users "made a Chief of Staff Bot responsible for coordinating several specialists" ([xAI: Designing Grok Bot](https://x.ai/news/designing-grok-bot), official). The gallery's many "Chief of Staff" Bots are user-made templates ([example](https://x.ai/bot/Pz17GKXbh7LKHkLu4IcR0), secondary). The docs' overview still links its "Morning briefing" item to a `#chief-of-staff` anchor that no longer exists, a trace of the rename ([docs: overview](https://docs.x.ai/grok-bot), official).
- **The design rule behind it:** "Capabilities and context therefore follow different boundaries in Grok Bot. Tools and Skills live at the account level because many Bots may need to browse the web, work with documents, or send email. Memory and Routines belong to the Bot because they reflect what that particular role knows and does over time." xAI "considered adding dashboards, assignment boards, and explicit handoff controls" and dropped them: "Each one gave the user more coordination work. Instead, coordinating Bots handle routine routing and bring the user in when a decision requires judgment." ([xAI: Designing Grok Bot](https://x.ai/news/designing-grok-bot), official)
- **Coordination shows up in the transcript as folded events:** "When a Bot creates a Routine, changes a setting, or messages another Bot, the event can appear directly in the transcript." The post's example: the user asks "Can you check in with everyone for me?", the coordinator replies "On it — pinging the team for status now", a folded row reads "6 messages with Kenny Tyler and Jenny", a three-line summary follows, and when the user asks for it every morning a "Created Routine Morning Briefing" event appears ([xAI: Designing Grok Bot](https://x.ai/news/designing-grok-bot), official).
- **What the coordinator can do to other Bots:** create them, hand them work, and report for them. We found no documented power to edit another Bot's memory or rules, or to pause or delete its routines; each routine belongs to one Bot and is changed "by asking the Bot in chat" ([docs: skills and routines](https://docs.x.ai/grok-bot/skills-routines-and-automations), official). Coordination is conversational, not administrative.
- **Bot-to-Bot messages:** "A Bot can send an asynchronous message to another Bot. The receiving Bot wakes, handles the request, and can reply later. You can see the handoff in the conversation." Handoffs land in the Bots' own chats, not in the group. A handoff "needs a Bot acting for one person on that turn": a personal Bot in the app may message other Bots, acting for you; in a shared Slack thread it may not ([docs: chat](https://docs.x.ai/grok-bot/chat-and-collaboration), official).
- **Loops and noise:** "There is no switch for this. Bots message each other to hand off work, and they follow your instructions about it." The fixes are instructions ("Don't message other Bots. Ask me first."), asking each Bot to remember them, and muting or hiding a helper. "Every Bot reply counts toward your usage, including replies in a group and messages between Bots." "Ask for a single owner at each stage. Parallel handoffs create duplicate work and noisy updates." ([docs: chat](https://docs.x.ai/grok-bot/chat-and-collaboration), official). A bug report shows the failure mode: Bots sent a bare "." instead of staying quiet, and a staff reply said that "in a group chat that sets off the other Bots, so it snowballs" ([Cursor forum, 2026-10-09](https://forum.cursor.com/t/grok-bots-keep-typing-single-in-chat/174148), secondary).
- **Memory:** "Each Bot has its own memory, but a few facts about you, such as your name and time zone, are shared by all your Bots." In a group, "your Bot's memory isn't shared with the other Bots there, but everyone in the group can read what your Bot says." There is no memory screen for a personal Bot; you ask it ("What do you remember about me?") ([docs: bots](https://docs.x.ai/grok-bot/bots), official). Context also crosses Bots through shared files in `/workspace`, browser sessions and handoffs ([docs: FAQ](https://docs.x.ai/grok-bot/faq), official).

**Safety**
- **Bots are roles, not security principals.** "The computer is assigned per user, not per Bot. Do not use separate Bots as a security boundary." ([docs: FAQ](https://docs.x.ai/grok-bot/faq), official). "Your Bots aren't separated from each other. Your other Bots are now signed in to the conference site too, and they can use the work card in Vault." ([docs: how it works](https://docs.x.ai/grok-bot/how-grok-bot-works), official)
- **Per-Bot permissions are thin.** Auto Review and Custom rules (up to 20 **Allow automatically** and 20 **Ask first**, 1,000 characters each, **Ask first** wins) are per account; connected apps are per account; the local-computer prompt reads "Allow Grok Bot and all Bots to run commands on your local computer?" ([docs: approvals](https://docs.x.ai/grok-bot/approvals-security-and-privacy), official). What is per Bot: **Disable drafts for Bot** (owner only, never in groups), the notification switch, and which private skills are enabled ([docs: chat](https://docs.x.ai/grok-bot/chat-and-collaboration), [docs: skills and routines](https://docs.x.ai/grok-bot/skills-routines-and-automations), official).
- **When a Bot acts on another Bot's request:** "Approvals from work that started without you, like a routine or a message from another Bot, expire after about 30 minutes. Approvals in a chat you are in wait for you." ([docs: FAQ](https://docs.x.ai/grok-bot/faq), official). Email from "any other turn you didn't start with that request, waits as a draft until you press **Send**, and there's no option for unattended sending" ([docs: computer and apps](https://docs.x.ai/grok-bot/computer-and-apps), official). In a group, "only the person whose message the Bot is answering can approve", and group cards offer no **Always allow** ([docs: approvals](https://docs.x.ai/grok-bot/approvals-security-and-privacy), official).
- **Injection boundary:** "Only your own messages count as instructions" ([docs: how it works](https://docs.x.ai/grok-bot/how-grok-bot-works), official); Auto Review weighs "what you asked for in your own messages. Text from somewhere else, such as an email or a web page, doesn't count as your request." ([docs: approvals](https://docs.x.ai/grok-bot/approvals-security-and-privacy), official). "Outside content is marked as untrusted data when presented to the model", under a network policy, per-action approvals and per-user isolation that "reduce, but do not eliminate, risk" ([docs: security](https://docs.x.ai/grok-bot/security), official). Whether a message from another of your Bots counts as your request is not stated (unverified); what is stated is that approvals it triggers expire like background work.

### 4.5 OpenClaw

The most documented multi-agent system here, and self-hosted. Read from the docs at docs.openclaw.ai and the repo's `docs/` folder on 2026-11-24. Two 2026 changes matter: since v2026.9.2 agents can read and message each other's sessions by default, and since v2026.11.5 the heartbeat is an ordinary scheduled job.

- **What an agent is:** "the full per-persona scope: workspace files, auth profiles, model registry, and session store"; a binding routes a channel account to one agent; the workspace is "not a hard sandbox" ([docs: multi-agent](https://docs.openclaw.ai/concepts/multi-agent), official). Its job lives in `AGENTS.md` and `SOUL.md`, its own `skills/` folder, shared Gateway plugins and MCP servers, and its automations ([docs: agents and templates](https://docs.openclaw.ai/concepts/agents-and-templates), official). Per-agent settings include `model`, `skills`, `identity {name, theme, emoji, avatar}`, `sandbox`, `tools` and `subagents` ([docs: agent entries](https://docs.openclaw.ai/gateway/config-agents/entries-and-multi-agent), official). Agents are created from the CLI (`openclaw agents add <id> --role ...`), from **New agent** in the Control UI through a setup chat, or as a team; an agent may ask for another, created "only after operator approval" ([docs: multi-agent](https://docs.openclaw.ai/concepts/multi-agent), official). No maximum is documented.
- **Chats:** every direct message "lands in one rolling conversation: the main session", shown as Home, with forks under Threads and group chats under Groups ([docs: main session](https://docs.openclaw.ai/concepts/main-session), official); scheduled jobs get a fresh session per run unless told otherwise ([docs: sessions](https://docs.openclaw.ai/concepts/session), official). Experimental "broadcast groups" run up to 16 agents on one message, one round by default (at most 4), and "All participants passing ends the thread" ([docs: broadcast groups](https://docs.openclaw.ai/channels/broadcast-groups), official).
- **Home and list:** **Agents** "shows the roster, current work status, and recent chat previews" ([docs: multi-agent](https://docs.openclaw.ai/concepts/multi-agent), official). "Choose **Pin** in an agent's options menu to move it into the icon section above" the roster; "Agent pins are saved in this browser profile". Sessions show unread dots and activity rings, can be pinned, archived and snoozed, and "An agent can also publish a short expiring status line and optionally request attention with a curated amber icon" ([docs: sessions and sidebar](https://docs.openclaw.ai/web/control-ui/sessions-and-sidebar), official). No pin limit is documented.
- **Scheduling:** schedules are `at` (a time or "20m"), `every` ("10m", "1h", "1d"), `cron` with a time zone, or a watched command's exit or output; jobs can be held to active hours ([docs: schedules](https://docs.openclaw.ai/automation/cron-jobs/schedules), official). The heartbeat became a normal job: "Fresh setup creates one **Proactive check** job for the ambient owner agent and leaves it disabled"; "Once enabled, it runs every 30 minutes" and "delivers to a resolved owner DM"; a check with nothing to say returns `NO_REPLY`; after the migration "repeated updates can be delivered" ([docs: heartbeat](https://docs.openclaw.ai/gateway/heartbeat), official). Results are announced to a chat, sent to a webhook or dropped ([docs: delivery](https://docs.openclaw.ai/automation/cron-jobs/delivery), official). Each job can be disabled or removed, and one switch stops all automatic runs ([repo: managing jobs](https://github.com/openclaw/openclaw/blob/main/docs/automation/cron-jobs/managing-jobs.md), official).
- **Coordinator:** a team preset "creates a chief of staff (`coordinator`), researcher, writer, and reviewer". "The chief of staff remains the human's point of contact: it discovers matching specialists, assigns bounded work, checks their artifacts, and reports a coherent result. Specialists return artifacts and evidence to the coordinator without delegating further." Delegation mode `"prefer"` "is prompt guidance, not a scheduler", and "Role instructions require human approval before external sends, publication, purchases, deletion, or production changes." ([docs: multi-agent](https://docs.openclaw.ai/concepts/multi-agent), official). The coordinator can spawn work only into agents listed in `subagents.allowAgents` (default: itself) and can stop its own children, which stops their whole tree ([docs: agent entries](https://docs.openclaw.ai/gateway/config-agents/entries-and-multi-agent), [docs: nesting](https://docs.openclaw.ai/tools/subagents/nesting), official).
- **Agent-to-agent messages:** `sessions_send` runs another session and can wait for its answer ([docs: session tools](https://docs.openclaw.ai/concepts/session-tool), official). Since v2026.10.1, "Each send returns one peer reply; automatic exchanges and target-channel posts are removed" ([repo: release notes](https://github.com/openclaw/openclaw/blob/main/docs/releases/2026.10.1.md), official). By default every agent can reach every other (`tools.agentToAgent.enabled: true`) and session visibility is `all`, a default changed in v2026.9.2 with a warning to review access ([docs: sessions and subagents](https://docs.openclaw.ai/gateway/config-tools/sessions-and-subagents), [docs: v2026.9.2](https://docs.openclaw.ai/releases/2026.9.2), official). Per agent, sends can be narrowed or set to `always`, `ask` or `never` ([docs: sessions config](https://docs.openclaw.ai/gateway/config-agents/sessions), official). The user sees them as compact "updates from" rows, not chat bubbles ([docs: Control UI](https://docs.openclaw.ai/web/control-ui), official).
- **Loop limits in code:** a bot pair on a chat channel may exchange 20 events in a 60-second window, then is suppressed for 60 seconds ([docs: bot loop protection](https://docs.openclaw.ai/channels/bot-loop-protection), official); a turn stops at the 20th identical tool call in a row ([docs: loop detection](https://docs.openclaw.ai/tools/loop-detection), official); sub-agents default to depth 5 and 5 children per agent, and hidden sub-agents lose `sessions_send`, `message` and `cron` ([docs: nesting](https://docs.openclaw.ai/tools/subagents/nesting), [docs: sub-agent tool policy](https://docs.openclaw.ai/tools/subagents/tool-policy), official).
- **Memory:** per agent ("each agent searches only its own configured memory"); shared material goes in extra paths ([docs: multi-agent](https://docs.openclaw.ai/concepts/multi-agent), official).
- **Safety:** per-agent sandbox, tool allow and deny lists in layers where "Each level can further restrict tools, but cannot grant back denied tools from earlier levels" ([docs: multi-agent sandbox and tools](https://docs.openclaw.ai/tools/multi-agent-sandbox-tools), official), and per-agent exec allowlists that "prevent one agent's approvals from leaking into others" ([docs: exec approvals](https://docs.openclaw.ai/tools/exec-approvals), official). Messages from another session are "marked as inter-session data in the receiving prompt (`[Inter-session message ... isUser=false]`)" and should be treated "as tool-routed data, not as a direct end-user-authored instruction"; a guest "never inherits the session owner's privileges" ([docs: session tools](https://docs.openclaw.ai/concepts/session-tool), official). That boundary was learned the hard way: advisory GHSA-w5c7-9qqw-6645 (February 2026) said "Inter-session prompts could be treated as direct user instructions" ([advisory](https://github.com/openclaw/openclaw/security/advisories/GHSA-w5c7-9qqw-6645), official). The prompt-injection page advises "Do not use older/weaker/smaller tiers for tool-enabled agents or untrusted inboxes" ([docs: prompt injection](https://docs.openclaw.ai/gateway/security/prompt-injection), official).
- **Reception:** praised as "a highly customizable and powerful AI assistant" ([The Batch](https://www.deeplearning.ai/the-batch/cutting-through-the-openclaw-and-moltbook-hype), secondary); a heartbeat "designed to verify every 30 minutes if tasks are pending ... sends the entire context window to the API", which cost one user "$18.75 gone in a single night" (early 2026, before the heartbeat became an opt-in job; [Notebookcheck](https://www.notebookcheck.net/18-75-overnight-to-ask-Is-it-daytime-yet-The-absurd-economics-of-OpenClaw-s-token-use.1219925.0.html), secondary); the heartbeat means "the agent can do consequential things before you've had a chance to weigh in" ([TechRadar](https://www.techradar.com/pro/here-are-the-openclaw-security-risks-you-should-know-about), secondary).

### 4.6 Atomic Agent

The closest analogue to Hermit: local-first, built for small quantized models, desktop and CLI with Telegram and Discord remotes. It has one agent, not a roster.

- **Bots:** "personas" are named instructions added to the agent's own; the Swarm tab runs extra Telegram or Discord bots, "each with its own token, owner, lockfile and sessions", whose `role` "is a free-text note shown in the tab and does not change what the bot does" ([README](https://github.com/AtomicBot-ai/atomic-agent/blob/main/README.md), official). There is no roster; chats have pins, folders, search and archive.
- **Scheduling:** `--at`, `--every` (minimum interval 1,000 ms) and `--cron`; "each recurring task gets one persistent session"; a heartbeat goes through a checklist "every 30 minutes by default, at least 5" ([docs: scheduling](https://atomicagent.io/docs/features/scheduling/), official). Interval drift is documented: "A `--every 3600` task whose turns take five minutes does not fire at :00 every hour. It fires roughly every 65 minutes" (same page).
- **Unattended approvals, two rules that conflict:** overnight runs say "Nobody is there to answer, so nobody is asked. An action that needs approval is denied and the run carries on without it", and the report lists those under **Needs you** ([docs: overnight](https://github.com/AtomicBot-ai/atomic-agent/blob/main/docs/user/overnight.md), official). But `task run` and `task tick` "run tasks at approval level 5: every tool call, including shell commands and file writes, is auto-approved" ([docs: scheduling](https://atomicagent.io/docs/features/scheduling/), official).
- **Coordination (Fusion):** "By default the orchestrator is a cloud model and the workers run on your local model" ([docs: architecture](https://atomicagent.io/docs/concepts/architecture/), official). "The orchestrator itself does not run mutating tools. Workers cannot delegate further, cannot reach you, and cannot schedule tasks or write memory." ([README](https://github.com/AtomicBot-ai/atomic-agent/blob/main/README.md), official). Workers that need the user end with a question that comes back to the orchestrator. Messages between personas or Swarm bots: not found.
- **How it fits small models:** "Atomic Agent is built around keeping each model call small and cheap so that long, multi-step sessions stay affordable on a quantized local model", with "One inference per step" ([docs: architecture](https://atomicagent.io/docs/concepts/architecture/), official); the prompt prefix stays "byte-stable inside a session so ... `cache_prompt` and slot pinning can reuse KV-cache"; "repeated identical tool calls draw a warning at 3 repeats and a hard veto at 5"; and it admits "Very small models emit malformed tool calls more often" ([README](https://github.com/AtomicBot-ai/atomic-agent/blob/main/README.md), official).
- **Reception:** praised for keeping state local and for approvals from Telegram, and called a developer preview ([Menon Lab](https://themenonlab.blog/blog/atomic-agent-local-first-ai-agent-gaia-benchmark), secondary); no independent forum threads found.

## 5. Patterns to borrow and patterns to avoid

### 5.1 Borrow

1. **A roster of jobs you can scan.** Each bot is a row with its name and a one-line status written as a result or a request ("Need your yes on the Friday all-hands deck"), and three attention states: needs you, new result, working. *From:* Grok Bot's sidebar and attention states, OpenClaw's roster with "current work status, and recent chat previews". *For Hermit:* rows with a line icon, the bot's name and its status line; amber for "needs you", as on the approval sheets.
2. **One designated coordinator as the front door.** New work starts there; it answers "what's new" for every bot; it suggests a new bot when a request keeps coming back "with its own sources, rules, or schedule". *From:* Grok Bot's starred primary Bot, OpenClaw's "chief of staff" preset that "remains the human's point of contact". *For Hermit:* the coordinator is Hermit's own chat, so the crab stays the single face and the other bots are its jobs.
3. **A shallow tree: the coordinator delegates, specialists don't.** Specialists "return artifacts and evidence to the coordinator without delegating further"; workers "cannot delegate further, cannot reach you, and cannot schedule tasks or write memory"; one send gets one reply. *From:* OpenClaw's team preset and its v2026.10.1 change, Atomic Agent's Fusion workers. *For Hermit:* depth one, enforced in code, which also caps how many model turns one request can cost.
4. **Permission lives outside the bots.** "Muse proposes actions, but only Sentinel can grant permission", and the approval appears "directly within the client UI — not via their conversation with Muse". Perplexity gives each teammate "its own isolated computer" with browsing sessions "limited to that teammate". *For Hermit:* the cage already plays Sentinel's role; with several bots it holds a grant list per bot (skills, connectors, vault items, wallet), and no bot, the coordinator included, can grant anything to another.
5. **Context per bot, a small shared profile.** Each bot keeps its own memory and schedules; a few facts about the user (name, time zone, language) are shared by all. *From:* Grok Bot ("Memory and Routines belong to the Bot"; name and time zone shared), OpenClaw's per-agent memory, dots' notes kept apart from ChatGPT memory.
6. **Schedules in plain words, read back, with a run history and a switch.** The user says "every weekday at 8"; the app shows it back in words with **Last ran**, **Next run**, recent results and a pause switch; schedules nobody looks at pause themselves. *From:* Grok Bot's **When to run** and Run history; dots' saved schedule (time zone, end date, which changes deserve a notification, where to deliver); auto-pause in ChatGPT, Gemini and Perplexity.
7. **Silence when nothing changed.** A wake that finds nothing new posts nothing. *From:* Perplexity ("If a run finds nothing new ... Computer stays quiet"), Muse ("notifies you only when something is meaningfully new or needs your input"), OpenClaw's and Atomic's `NO_REPLY`. *For Hermit:* essential for hourly bots; the run history still records the quiet run.
8. **A quiet front door, details elsewhere.** "Your main thread stays quieter: Claude Duet posts there when a task is done or needs a decision from you." Delegations fold into one row ("6 messages with Kenny Tyler and Jenny") that opens to the exchange. *From:* Claude Duet's Tasks tab, Grok Bot's transcript events, OpenClaw's "updates from" rows. *For Hermit:* each handoff is also an entry in the signed audit log.
9. **Work the user didn't start can't commit anything.** Background approvals expire; unattended email stays a draft; proactive research is read-only; a standing approval names "the action, recipients, trigger, and schedule". *From:* Grok Bot's 30-minute expiry and drafts, dots' read-only proactive research and scoped automations, Atomic Agent's overnight runs that deny and list under **Needs you**. *For Hermit:* the night shift's rule (reads only; everything else becomes a morning suggestion) applies to every turn a bot starts on a schedule or on another bot's request.
10. **Only the user's words are instructions.** Messages from other bots, people, pages and emails are data. *From:* OpenClaw marks them `[Inter-session message ... isUser=false]` to be treated "as tool-routed data", after an advisory found "Inter-session prompts could be treated as direct user instructions"; Grok Bot ("Only your own messages count as instructions"); dots ("Only the owner can direct their personal dot"). *For Hermit:* another bot's message enters the cage as untrusted input that taints the turn, and the sheet's "why" line quotes the user, never a bot.
11. **Per-bot notification switches and starter bots.** "Get notified when this Bot finishes or is waiting for you", per bot; templates for common jobs; Duplicate that keeps the role and schedules but not the memory. *From:* Grok Bot.

### 5.2 Avoid

1. **Bots that look separate but share everything.** Grok Bot's bots share one computer, its logins and the saved card: "Do not use separate Bots as a security boundary." *For Hermit:* per-bot grants enforced by the cage (5.1 item 4). The coordinator does not inherit its specialists' grants.
2. **Bot-to-bot traffic with no hard stop.** Grok Bot has "no switch" and relies on instructions; a bug made Bots answer each other with "." until it "snowballs". *For Hermit:* OpenClaw's approach, in code: one reply per send, a pair limit (20 events a minute there; far fewer on a phone), a hop limit per request, a daily turn budget per bot, and a visible switch for bots messaging each other.
3. **Open by default.** Since OpenClaw v2026.9.2 every agent can reach every other and read other agents' transcripts unless narrowed. *For Hermit:* closed by default; the user links bots explicitly, and a bot sees another bot's results, never its memory or history.
4. **Proactivity you can't turn off.** Grok Bot's check-ins can only be disabled through support. *For Hermit:* a switch, off for new bots, as OpenClaw leaves its "Proactive check" job disabled.
5. **Two rules for unattended work.** Atomic Agent's overnight runs deny anything needing approval, while its scheduled tasks run "at approval level 5: every tool call ... is auto-approved". *For Hermit:* one rule for every turn nobody watched.
6. **Costs you only see afterwards.** An hourly Grok Bot routine "can use a week of usage in a day"; an early-2026 OpenClaw heartbeat "sends the entire context window to the API" every 30 minutes. *For Hermit:* the cost is battery, heat and time; build each wake's prompt from the bot's notebook, show each bot's phone time this week, and skip wakes when the phone is hot or low.
7. **Rosters without limits, and a character per bot.** Users reach 17 Grok Bots and ask for folders to hold "a coordinator plus specialists". Hermit's bots take turns on one model, so a small cap keeps each one useful. Hermit's brand rule is one crab, never multiplied (`docs/research/design-benchmark.md` §6.8): bots get names and line icons, not mascots.
8. **Groups where every bot answers.** Grok Bot's default lets the bots "decide who should respond", and every reply costs. *For Hermit:* in a group the coordinator answers; another bot speaks only when named, as Character.AI's "Tap a Character to chat" leaves the choice to the user.
9. **Memory you can only see by asking, and threads that grow forever.** Grok Bot has "no memory screen for a personal Bot"; dots' single thread gets long and "Search doesn't work with your Dot's thread". *For Hermit:* each bot's memory as readable entries (S5 in the design benchmark), prompts built from the notebook and recent turns, and searchable history.
10. **A Pause that doesn't pause.** dots' Pause "doesn't stop every delegated task or cancel future scheduled runs". *For Hermit:* pausing a bot stops its schedules and queued messages, and one control stops all bots.

### 5.3 The five that matter most for Hermit

1. **Hermit is the coordinator; specialist bots are its jobs, one level deep.** One front door, one crab, delegation only from the coordinator to specialists, enforced in code (5.1 items 2 and 3).
2. **Each bot is a real boundary.** A grant list per bot in the cage; no shared logins, vault items or wallet by default; nothing a bot can grant another (5.1 item 4, 5.2 items 1 and 3).
3. **A bot's message is data, and any turn the user didn't start is unattended.** Reads only; writes become suggestions; send, pay and log in only with the user present, the sheet and the fingerprint (5.1 items 9 and 10).
4. **Hard limits on bots talking to each other.** One reply per send, a hop limit, a daily turn budget, and a visible switch (5.2 item 2).
5. **Schedules that tell the truth on a phone.** Plain words read back, Last ran and Next run, silence when nothing changed, auto-pause when unused, and one small step per wake (5.1 items 6 and 7, section 6).

## 6. What an on-device, single-model phone app can and can't copy

Every product above runs its bots on hardware that is always on: cloud computers (Grok Bot shares one per user; Perplexity gives one to each teammate; dots, Cue and Manus have their own), the vendor's servers and virtual machines (Duet, Muse), or a gateway or desktop the user keeps running (OpenClaw, Atomic Agent). Hermit runs one 0.8B model on the phone and has no server. Each multi-bot feature has to be mapped onto what Android and one small model allow.

### 6.1 Hourly wake-ups versus Android's battery and thermal limits

**What Android allows (official):**
- **WorkManager's floor is 15 minutes, and it is a floor, not a clock:** "The minimum repeat interval that can be defined is 15 minutes"; the interval is "the minimum time between repetitions", the exact time "depends on the constraints ... and on the optimizations performed by the system", and with a charging constraint a run can be "delayed, or even skipped" ([Android: define work](https://developer.android.com/develop/background-work/background-tasks/persistent/getting-started/define-work)).
- **Doze stops background work:** when the phone is unplugged, still and dark, Android "Suspends network access", "Ignores wake locks", defers alarms and "Doesn't let `JobScheduler` run ... so `WorkManager` tasks don't run"; maintenance windows come "less frequently" the longer the phone sits; and even while-idle alarms fire no more than "once per nine minutes, per app" ([Android: Doze and App Standby](https://developer.android.com/training/monitoring-device-state/doze-standby)).
- **Standby buckets cap the total time:** regular jobs get "Up to 20 minutes in a rolling 60 minute period" in the active bucket, 10 minutes per 4 hours in working set, per 12 hours in frequent, per 24 hours with no network in rare, and "Once per day for up to 10 minutes" in restricted. On the charger there are "No execution limits except for restricted standby bucket". Since Android 16, jobs that run while the app holds a foreground service count against these quotas ([Android: power limits](https://developer.android.com/topic/performance/power/power-details)).
- **Long runs need a foreground service, and starting one from the background is mostly banned:** apps targeting Android 12 or later "can't start foreground services while the app is running in the background", with exceptions that include "The user turns off battery optimizations for your app" and an exact alarm "to complete an action that the user requests" ([Android: background starts](https://developer.android.com/develop/background-work/services/fgs/restrictions-bg-start)); a `dataSync` service gets "a total of 6 hours in a 24-hour period" ([Android: FGS timeout](https://developer.android.com/develop/background-work/services/fgs/timeout)).
- **Exact alarms are gated:** `SCHEDULE_EXACT_ALARM` "is not pre-granted to fresh installs" of apps targeting Android 13 or later, and `USE_EXACT_ALARM` has "Limited use cases" ([Android: alarms](https://developer.android.com/develop/background-work/services/alarms/schedule)). Google Play forbids asking for a direct Doze exemption "unless the core function of the app is adversely affected"; a "Task automation app" is listed as acceptable ([Android: Doze and App Standby](https://developer.android.com/training/monitoring-device-state/doze-standby)).
- **Heat:** `getThermalHeadroom` "predicts how long the device can maintain the current performance level" and should be read at most every 10 seconds ([Android: thermal](https://developer.android.com/games/optimize/adpf/thermal)).
- **No push wake-ups:** Android's way to wake an idle app is a Firebase Cloud Messaging message from a server ([Android: Doze and App Standby](https://developer.android.com/training/monitoring-device-state/doze-standby)). Hermit has no server, so every wake must come from the phone's own scheduler. The cloud products wake their bots on their servers and only notify the phone.

**What Hermit already has:** the night shift wakes through a periodic WorkManager job every 15 minutes while charging, gated to the night window, which starts a `dataSync` foreground service; when Android refuses the service, the pass runs inside the worker under its ten-minute limit (Q69, Q75 in `docs/build/open-questions.md`). It pauses at Android's moderate thermal status (Q65). That is the engine multi-bot wake-ups should reuse.

**What an hourly schedule can mean on a phone:**
- **On the charger** (overnight, at a desk), hourly or faster is realistic: "connecting a charger" ends Doze ([Android: Doze and App Standby](https://developer.android.com/training/monitoring-device-state/doze-standby)), job limits lift, and bots can take turns inside the night shift's windows.
- **Heat still wins.** Charging warms the phone, and the night shift already pauses at Android's moderate thermal status (Q65), so an hourly wake can be skipped even on the charger; the run history should say why ("Skipped: phone warm").
- **On battery with the screen off,** an hourly schedule becomes at most hourly, when Android allows: Doze holds jobs until a maintenance window, and a phone the user rarely opens drops to buckets with minutes of job time per day.
- **A wake is small.** BotPsy turns took 12 to 52 s on the Galaxy S26 Ultra, most of it reading a 4,400-token prompt ([phone session](../build/phone-session-2026-11-24.md)), and reloading the model costs about 2 s and drops the prompt cache (Q181). A ten-minute worker window fits roughly 11 to 50 such turns, shared by every bot due at that time, so a wake has room for a few small steps, not an hour of research.
- **So:** offer schedules in words that are true on Android ("every hour while charging", "a few times a day", "each night"), show **Last ran** and **Next run** per bot as Grok Bot's Run history does, use the reminder path (exact alarms the user already granted) only for things the user asked to happen at a time, and let a bot that missed its hour run at the next window instead of stacking runs.

### 6.2 One model loaded at a time, so bots take turns

- **A bot is a profile, not a process.** Cloud bots run in parallel ("Several Bots can therefore use browser and desktop tools in parallel", [Grok Bot docs](https://docs.x.ai/grok-bot/computer-and-apps)). Hermit has one engine slot (`apps/mobile/src/engine-slot.ts`) and one model of about 530 MB (`docs/research/botpsy.md`). A Hermit bot is a name, a job, a memory slice, a skill allowlist and a schedule over the same model.
- **One queue, the user first.** Grok Bot's rule fits: "A direct message from you takes priority over background work" ([docs: chat](https://docs.x.ai/grok-bot/chat-and-collaboration)). Order: the user's turn, then due wakes, then messages between bots. A bot-to-bot message is a queue entry with a hop budget, not a live conversation.
- **Switching bots costs a prompt read.** Each bot's instructions and memory make a different prompt, and the phone spends 8 to 20 s per model call reading prompts of about 4,400 tokens ([phone session](../build/phone-session-2026-11-24.md)). Keep the shared part (tools, cage rules) identical and first, the bot's own part short and last, so one prefix cache serves every bot (`docs/research/device-adaptive-runtime.md` covers the cache patch).
- **Group chats multiply cost.** Grok Bot says "fewer Bots in a group means fewer replies". On the phone a group of four bots each answering is four sequential model turns, close to a minute. A coordinator that answers once from the other bots' saved results is the phone-sized version.

### 6.3 The small model's limits on long multi-step research

- **The model card names the weak spots:** open-ended search (BrowseComp-Plus 4.5, BFCL web search 14.0) and memory (BFCL memory 16.3) ([BotPsy-0.8B card](https://huggingface.co/qvac/BotPsy-0.8B), via `docs/research/botpsy.md`). Hermit's web spike measured 38 to 54% per step when the model drives a page and 0% on a reorder (Q123), and no web navigation result below 2B has been published (`docs/research/web-on-phone.md`).
- **Errors compound:** at 90% per step, ten dependent steps all succeed about 35% of the time (0.9^10). An hourly bot that keeps a long investigation going by itself is exactly the shape where a 0.8B model drifts without anyone noticing.
- **What a phone research bot can do:** keep the research plan and the findings in code-owned structures (a list of open questions and sources to check, and a notebook of typed facts, each with its source), and do one bounded step per wake. Code fetches; the quarantined reader extracts typed records; the model picks the next item from a short list and writes one line of progress. The notebook, not the chat history, is the bot's memory, which also keeps prompts short.
- **A travel bot, for example:** comparing trips needs search forms, logins, and cross-checking dates and prices across many sites. That is open-ended multi-site browsing, which Hermit already assigns to Boost (Q20, Q37). On the phone the bot can keep the trip as structured records, run known lookups, and list what to check next; the long research runs on Boost.

### 6.4 Which tasks need the user's computer (Boost) or the web

| Task | On the phone | Needs the network | Needs Boost |
|---|---|---|---|
| Coordinator's digest of what each bot did | Yes, from the bots' saved results; code assembles it, the model writes a few lines | No | No |
| Coordinator delegating and checking status | Yes, as queue entries and typed results | No | No |
| Hourly research step on a known source | Yes, one bounded step per wake | Yes (fetch) | No |
| Open-ended research across sites, with logins and synthesis | No | Yes | Yes |
| A running coach reading the week's runs | Partly: code reads the fitness service's data, the model narrates | Yes (the service's API or site, a token in the vault) | For analysis that needs long context or many months |
| A travel bot changing a booking | Only with the user present: approval sheet and fingerprint; at night it becomes a morning suggestion | Yes | No |
| Sending, paying, logging in for any bot | Only with the user present, never on a schedule | Yes | No |

The last two rows are where Hermit is already stricter than the cloud products, and they converge on the same rule from the other side: Grok Bot keeps email from routines and other unattended turns as drafts with "no option for unattended sending" and lets approvals from background work expire after about 30 minutes ([docs: computer and apps](https://docs.x.ai/grok-bot/computer-and-apps), [docs: FAQ](https://docs.x.ai/grok-bot/faq)).

## 7. Gaps and what we could not verify

- **Grok Bot:** whether pinning has a cap (the docs give no number); whether a message from another of your Bots counts as "your request" for Auto Review (the docs only say its approvals expire like background work); how often the primary Bot checks in (no interval documented); the changelog at x.ai/bot/changelog was blocked (HTTP 403), and the grok.com help pages for routines and plans rendered without content.
- **dots:** a fixed list of schedule frequencies; unread badges on the dot's tab; whether Microsoft Teams messaging shipped (the launch post names it, the current channel docs don't).
- **ChatGPT Tasks:** the frequency floor differs by surface (hourly in the release notes, minute-based for tasks inside a chat), and we could not reconcile them.
- **OpenClaw:** the turn limits that applied to agent-to-agent exchanges before v2026.10.1; how the agent roster is ordered.
- **Atomic Agent:** which of its two unattended approval rules applies to tasks scheduled from the desktop app; the desktop app's source is not public, so its chip labels are unverified.
- **Claude Duet:** how channels are created, whether they take their own instructions, any cap on channels, the exact check-in interval, and how a routine is stopped; none of these is in Anthropic's pages.
- **Muse:** Meta's help-center pages render in the browser and returned only their titles, so the reminder frequencies ("daily, weekly, or on a custom interval"), Goals and side-chat steps come from search summaries; caps on side chats, goals and scheduled tasks; whether sub-agents appear in the activity log.
- **Cue by Manus:** the number of agents allowed, the home screen layout, pinning, routine frequencies, where routine results land, and whether memory is shared between agents; the Google Play listing didn't render for our tools.
- **Perplexity Teammates:** a cap on teammates, pinning, and how messages between teammates appear to the user.
- **Poe and Character.AI:** both help centers were blocked (HTTP 403); turn-taking in Poe group chats and Character.AI's current group limits come from press and guides only.
- **Gemini:** a cap on Gems before their removal, and when the multi-agent "Gemini agent" reaches consumers.
- **Store screenshots versus text:** the earlier design benchmark read some strings from store screenshots (Cue's "Memory updated", "S$32 below your limit"); no text source confirms them.
